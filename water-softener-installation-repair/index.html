Signs You Need A Softener

How Hard Water Shows Up At Home

Spots On Dishes And Glassware

Mineral deposits leave visible spotting even after washing and drying.

Dry Skin And Hair

Hard water can leave a mineral film that affects how soap and shampoo rinse away.

Reduced Appliance Lifespan

Water heaters and washing machines accumulate scale that reduces efficiency and shortens service life.

Soap Not Lathering Well

Hard water reacts with soap, reducing lather and requiring more product for the same result.

Common Repair Issues

When A Softener Needs Service

Salt Bridging

A hardened salt crust can form in the brine tank, preventing proper regeneration even when salt levels look adequate.

Resin Bed Fouling

Sediment or iron in the water supply can foul the resin bed over time, reducing softening capacity.

Control Valve Failure

The valve that manages regeneration cycles can wear out or malfunction, leading to inconsistent softening.

Signs It Needs Service

A return of hard water symptoms, like spotting or reduced lather, despite the system running usually means it's time for a checkup.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if my water is actually hard?

We test your water on site to measure actual hardness levels rather than guessing from symptoms alone.

Is a salt-based or salt-free system better?

It depends on your priorities. Salt-based systems remove hardness minerals directly, while salt-free systems condition water without removing them.

How much maintenance does a softener need?

Salt-based systems need periodic salt refills, while salt-free systems generally need less routine attention.

Will a softener fix low water pressure too?

No, a softener addresses water hardness, not pressure. A separate pressure issue would need its own diagnosis.

Can a softener be installed alongside a filtration system?

Yes, softening and filtration solve different problems and pair well together, typically installed in sequence at the main line.
