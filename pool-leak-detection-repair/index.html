Is It Really A Leak

Ruling Out Evaporation First

The Bucket Test

Comparing water loss inside a bucket against the pool's actual drop over 24 hours separates evaporation from a real leak.

Time Of Year Matters

Fort Lauderdale's hot, dry stretches naturally increase evaporation, which can be mistaken for a leak if not accounted for.

Equipment Pad Wetness

A consistently wet or soggy spot around the pump, filter, or heater often points to a plumbing leak at that equipment.

Air In The Lines

Air bubbling from a return line or a pump that struggles to prime can indicate a suction-side leak.

Where Pool Leaks Happen

Common Leak Locations

Skimmer Line Cracks

Ground settling around the skimmer can crack the connecting pipe over time, a common issue in South Florida's sandy soil.

Equipment Pad Fittings

Threaded fittings at the pump, filter, or heater can loosen or crack from years of pressure and vibration.

Return Line Breaks

Lines running from the equipment pad to pool returns can crack from ground shifting or root intrusion.

Light Niche Conduits

The electrical conduit for a pool light can develop a leak path if not properly sealed originally.

Plumbing Vs. Structural Leaks

Why This Distinction Matters

Plumbing Leaks

Leaks in the pipes running to and from the pool are what we handle, typically a more contained and less expensive repair.

Structural Leaks

A crack in the pool shell itself is a different specialty, usually handled by a pool contractor rather than a plumber.

How We Tell The Difference

Pressure testing the plumbing lines in isolation from the shell tells us definitively which category the leak falls into.

Referral When Needed

If testing points to a structural issue, we'll tell you plainly rather than guessing at a plumbing fix that won't solve it.

Fort Lauderdale's sandy soil and high water table make ground settling around pool equipment and skimmer lines a genuinely common cause of plumbing leaks here, more so than in areas with more stable clay-based soil. Repairs follow the plumbing provisions of the Florida Building Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

FAQs

Common Questions

How much water loss is normal from evaporation?

Roughly a quarter to half an inch per day is common in Fort Lauderdale's climate, more during hot, windy, or dry stretches. Loss beyond that usually points to a leak.

Do you dig up my whole pool deck to find a leak?

No, pressure testing narrows the location first, so any digging is targeted rather than exploratory.

Can a pool leak damage my home's foundation?

A significant, ongoing leak near a home's foundation can contribute to soil erosion issues over time, which is part of why prompt repair matters.

Is this the same as a pool resurfacing or shell repair?

No, we handle the plumbing lines specifically. A shell crack or resurfacing need would go to a pool contractor instead.
