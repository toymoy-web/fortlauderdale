Signs Of A Main Line Problem

When It's The Service Line, Not Interior Plumbing

Low Pressure Throughout The House

If every fixture is affected, not just one, the issue is likely upstream at the main line.

Wet Spot Between Meter And House

A soggy area of yard along the likely path of the service line often means an underground leak.

Unusually High Water Bill

A leak in the buried main line can waste significant water before it ever becomes visible above ground.

Discolored Water At Every Tap

Sediment or rust affecting the whole house often points to the main line itself, not a single fixture.

Repair Vs. Replacement

What Determines The Right Approach

Localized Damage

A single break or crack in an otherwise sound line is often a candidate for spot repair.

Widespread Corrosion

Old galvanized service lines corroding throughout their length usually call for full replacement.

Trenchless Replacement

A damaged line can often be replaced by pulling new pipe through the old path, minimizing yard disruption.

Material Upgrade

Replacement is a chance to upgrade from old galvanized pipe to modern copper or PEX.

Whose Line Is It

Where Responsibility Splits

Utility Side

The utility generally owns and maintains the line from the main up to the meter, including the meter itself.

Property Owner Side

Everything from the meter into the house is typically the homeowner's responsibility, including repair or replacement costs.

Confirm Before Assuming

Boundary details vary slightly by jurisdiction, so we confirm the specific split for your address before quoting.

Permit Coordination

Work in the right-of-way near the street sometimes requires coordination with the municipality, which we handle as part of the project.

Older Fort Lauderdale properties, particularly in historic neighborhoods, sometimes still run original galvanized service lines from the street. These are well past the point where spot repair makes more sense than a full trenchless replacement.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if it's the main line and not my house plumbing?

Whole-house symptoms, like low pressure or discoloration at every tap, point toward the main line rather than an isolated interior pipe.

Will replacing the main line tear up my whole yard?

Trenchless replacement can often pull new pipe through the existing path, avoiding a full trench in many cases.

Who is responsible for the line, me or the utility?

Typically the property owner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house, while the utility maintains the line up to the meter.

Can a small leak in the main line really raise my bill that much?

Yes, a buried leak can run continuously and go unnoticed for a long time, adding up significantly before it's found.
