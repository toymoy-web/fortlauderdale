Filter Change Safety

What To Know Before Servicing A System

Shut Off Water First

Always close the system's isolation valve before opening a filter housing to avoid a sudden pressurized release.

Relieve Pressure Before Opening

Most housings have a small pressure-release button or valve. Use it before removing the housing to prevent a mess or minor injury from a stuck lid.

Check O-Rings Each Change

A cracked or missing O-ring is the most common cause of a leak after a filter change.

Know Your System's Bypass

Most whole-home systems have a bypass valve so water service continues during maintenance, worth locating before you need it.

What A System Actually Removes

Understanding Filtration Media

Carbon Filtration

Activated carbon is the workhorse of most whole-home systems, effective against chlorine taste, odor, and many organic compounds.

Sediment Pre-Filters

A sediment stage ahead of carbon protects the carbon media and extends its usable life between changes.

Reverse Osmosis Membranes

RO systems push water through a semi-permeable membrane, removing a broader range of dissolved substances than carbon alone.

UV Treatment

Some systems add a UV stage as an extra layer, though it's rarely necessary given Fort Lauderdale's already-treated municipal supply.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is Fort Lauderdale's tap water actually safe to drink?

Yes, municipal water meets federal and state safety standards. Filtration here primarily improves taste, smell, and clarity rather than addressing a safety issue.

What's the difference between filtration and a water softener?

Filtration targets taste, odor, and sediment, while a softener specifically addresses mineral hardness. Many homes benefit from both.

How often do filters need to be changed?

It depends on the system and household water use, typically every 3-12 months depending on filter type.

Can I install just an under-sink system instead of whole-home?

Yes, an under-sink system is a lower-cost option focused specifically on drinking and cooking water.

Will filtration reduce the mineral content that affects hardness?

Standard filtration targets taste and sediment, not hardness minerals specifically. Softening is the right tool for hardness, and the two are often paired.
