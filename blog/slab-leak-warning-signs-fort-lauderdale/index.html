Slab Leak Warning Signs Every Fort Lauderdale Homeowner Should Know

A warm spot on the floor or an unexplained bill spike could mean a leak under your slab. Here's what to watch for.

Slab leaks are one of the more disruptive plumbing problems a Fort Lauderdale homeowner can face, mostly because they happen somewhere you can't see: under the concrete foundation itself. Most South Florida homes are built slab-on-grade, so understanding the warning signs matters more here than in regions where plumbing runs through an accessible basement.

Historic Neighborhoods And Original Copper

Homes in Victoria Park, Colee Hammock, and Rio Vista are among the most likely in Fort Lauderdale to still run original copper supply lines from construction decades ago. That doesn't mean every older home has a slab leak waiting to happen. It does mean the statistical odds shift meaningfully once a copper system passes the several-decade mark, especially for a property within a mile or two of the coast or canal system where corrosion runs faster than it would inland.

If your home falls into that profile and you haven't had a plumber assess the supply line material and general condition, it's a reasonable thing to ask about during any other service visit, even one unrelated to a suspected leak.

What Actually Causes A Slab Leak

Aging Copper Pipe

Copper supply lines installed decades ago are reaching the point where pinhole leaks become statistically more likely.

Salt Air Corrosion

Homes closer to the coast and Fort Lauderdale's canal system see faster copper pipe corrosion than inland Florida properties.

Ground Shifting

Soil movement, sometimes from irrigation or drainage changes, can stress a pipe at a joint until it cracks.

Poor Original Installation

In some cases, a line installed with a sharp bend or contact against rebar during construction wears through years earlier than it should.

Signs Worth Taking Seriously

A warm spot on the floor, especially on tile, is one of the most recognizable signs of a hot water line leaking under the slab. A sudden increase in your water bill with no change in usage is another. Some homeowners also report hearing the sound of running water in the floor when every fixture in the house is off, which is worth investigating immediately rather than waiting to see if it resolves on its own.

Less obvious signs include a persistent musty smell in one room, cracked tile or baseboards that seem to have shifted for no clear reason, or mold growth along a wall near the floor. Any of these, especially combined with a higher water bill, is worth a professional look.

How Detection Actually Works

Contrary to what many homeowners assume, finding a slab leak doesn't require cutting into the floor to search for it. Electronic leak detection uses acoustic listening equipment to pick up the sound of water escaping under pressure, along with thermal imaging that can spot a temperature difference at the surface caused by a hot water line leak underneath. Once the location is narrowed down, only that specific section needs to be opened, if opening anything is even necessary.

Repair Or Reroute

Once a slab leak is confirmed, there are generally two paths. The line can be repaired in place, which usually means breaking through the slab at the leak's exact location. Alternatively, the damaged section can be rerouted above the slab, through walls or attic space, which avoids breaking concrete entirely but does mean some new visible pipe depending on the home's layout. Which option makes more sense depends on the leak's location, the home's construction, and how the homeowner feels about each trade-off. Either approach follows the plumbing provisions of the Florida Building Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

What To Do If You Suspect A Slab Leak Right Now

Note What You've Noticed

Write down when you first saw the warm spot, heard the sound, or noticed the bill increase. That timeline helps with diagnosis.

Don't Cut Into The Slab Yourself

Attempting to locate or expose a slab leak without detection equipment risks damaging the wrong section of flooring and structure.

Watch For Mold Or A Musty Odor

Sustained moisture under flooring can lead to mold growth. A musty smell alongside a suspected leak is worth mentioning when you call.

Know Your Main Shutoff

If the leak is significant, shutting off the main water valve limits further water loss until a technician arrives.

What A Slab Leak Costs Versus What Waiting Costs

Homeowners often delay a slab leak repair because the price of opening a floor sounds worse than a slightly elevated water bill. In practice, the math usually runs the other way. A slow leak left unaddressed for months doesn't just waste water; it can saturate soil under the foundation, stain or warp flooring, and in coastal, humid climates like Fort Lauderdale's, create the exact conditions mold needs to take hold behind a wall or under cabinetry. Addressing a confirmed leak promptly, even if the fix itself takes a day or two, is almost always the cheaper outcome once those secondary costs are counted.

FAQs

Is a slab leak always an emergency?

Not always, but it should be addressed quickly. A slow leak can still cause significant water loss and, eventually, damage to flooring or the slab itself if left unaddressed.

Will my insurance cover a slab leak?

Most Florida homeowner policies cover the cost to access and repair a sudden slab leak, but not the water bill itself or gradual damage that built up over time. Check your specific policy language.

Can one slab leak mean more are coming?

If the cause is age-related corrosion in copper pipe, other sections of the same system have seen similar wear and are worth monitoring.

Can a slab leak affect my home's foundation?

A leak left unaddressed for a long period can contribute to soil erosion or settling issues under the affected area, which is part of why prompt repair matters beyond just the water bill.

How long does a typical slab leak repair take?

An in-place repair is often completed in a single day once the location is confirmed, while a full reroute can take a bit longer depending on the home's layout and how much pipe needs to be run.
