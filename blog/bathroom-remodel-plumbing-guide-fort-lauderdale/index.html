Bathroom Remodel Plumbing: What To Expect In Fort Lauderdale

The plumbing behind the walls makes or breaks a bathroom remodel. Here's what the scope actually covers and what older homes here often reveal.

A bathroom remodel lives or dies on the plumbing behind the walls, and it's the part of the project most homeowners understand least going in, right up until something unexpected surfaces mid-demolition. Here's what actually happens on the plumbing side, and what tends to catch people off guard in Fort Lauderdale specifically.

What The Plumbing Scope Actually Covers

Fixture Relocation

Moving a toilet, sink, or shower to a new position in the room means fully rerouting supply and drain lines, not just reinstalling fixtures in new spots.

Rough-In Plumbing

A fully gutted bathroom needs supply, drain, and vent lines fully installed and pressure-tested before walls and flooring go back in.

Venting Requirements

Every fixture needs proper venting to drain correctly, and a new layout sometimes requires rethinking the entire vent path rather than a small adjustment.

Permit And Inspection

Significant plumbing changes typically require a permit and a formal inspection at the rough-in stage, before anything gets closed up.

What Older Fort Lauderdale Homes Often Reveal

Once demolition starts in a home built before the 1970s, it's genuinely common to find original cast iron drain lines or galvanized supply lines behind the existing fixtures. This shows up most often in neighborhoods like Victoria Park, Colee Hammock, and Rio Vista. It isn't necessarily a problem, but it does change the project scope if that plumbing needs updating as part of the remodel rather than being left in place. A contractor who plans for this possibility in the initial quote handles it far better than one who treats it as an unavoidable surprise change order once the walls are already open and the clock is running.

Layout Changes Cost More Than They Look Like They Should

Moving a toilet three feet to accommodate a new layout sounds simple, but it means relocating the drain connection, which often means breaking into a slab in a South Florida home built slab-on-grade. Keeping fixtures near their existing plumbing locations is almost always the more budget-friendly path, even if it means compromising slightly on the ideal layout. This is worth discussing with your plumber before finalizing a design with a contractor or designer, since the plumbing cost difference between two layout options can be substantial.

Waterproofing Matters More Here Than It Might Elsewhere

South Florida's humidity means a poorly waterproofed shower pan or an improperly sealed penetration through a wall has less margin for error than it would in a drier climate. Moisture that gets behind tile or under a threshold doesn't dry out on its own the way it might in an arid region; it sits there, and over time that creates the conditions mold needs to take hold. A remodel that skips proper waterproofing membrane behind a tiled shower, or cuts corners on sealing where plumbing penetrates a wall, is setting up a problem that often doesn't show itself until well after the contractor has moved on to the next job. All plumbing work follows the plumbing provisions of the Florida Building Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

What To Do After The Remodel Is Complete

Watch For Water Behind Fixtures In The First Weeks

Check around the base of new fixtures periodically for any sign of moisture during the first several weeks of normal use.

Ventilate The Bathroom Regularly

Running a fan or opening a window after showers helps prevent the humidity buildup that leads to mold in a newly finished space.

Let New Grout And Caulk Fully Cure

Avoid heavy water exposure until sealant has cured per the product's stated time, to prevent early moisture intrusion behind the surround.

Report Anything Unusual Promptly

A soft spot in the floor or discoloration on a ceiling below the bathroom should be reported right away, not monitored over time.

Planning The Project Timeline

Design Review

A plumber reviews the planned layout against existing lines to flag complications before demolition starts.

Demolition And Assessment

Once walls are open, the actual condition of existing plumbing is confirmed.

Rough-In And Inspection

New supply, drain, and vent lines are installed and inspected before walls close up.

Final Fixture Connection

Once tile and finishes are complete, fixtures are connected and tested.

Popular Upgrades Worth Planning For Early

A curbless, fully waterproofed shower needs specific drain slope and placement decided before any tile goes down, not adjusted afterward. A freestanding tub needs floor-mounted or wall-mounted fill valves positioned in advance. A double vanity conversion from a single sink typically means adding a second drain and supply set within existing wall framing. Each of these is straightforward when planned during the rough-in phase and considerably more disruptive if decided after the fact.

Budgeting For The Unexpected

A reasonable rule of thumb for any older-home remodel is setting aside a contingency for plumbing discoveries once demolition starts, rather than assuming the initial quote covers every possibility. A home with confirmed original cast iron or galvanized supply lines is more likely to need that contingency than a home already known to have updated plumbing. Discussing this possibility with your plumber before the project starts, rather than after a wall is already open, keeps the conversation about options rather than a forced decision made under time pressure.

FAQs

How long does the plumbing portion of a remodel typically take?

A rough-in for a single bathroom is often a day or two, with a separate final connection visit once finishes are complete.

Can you work alongside our general contractor?

Yes, we regularly coordinate with contractors and designers, fitting our timeline into the broader project schedule rather than working in isolation.

What if you find old plumbing we didn't know about?

We explain what we found and quote any additional work before proceeding, rather than treating it as an unavoidable surprise charge.

Do we need a permit for a bathroom remodel?

Significant plumbing changes typically require one, and we handle filing as part of the project.

Can the plumbing work start before our tile is chosen?

Yes, rough-in plumbing generally proceeds based on fixture placement and type rather than final tile selection, though drain and valve locations should be confirmed before rough-in begins.
