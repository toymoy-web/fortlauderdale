Why Is My Water Bill So High? Common Causes We See in Fort Lauderdale

Before assuming your rates went up, run this quick meter check to find out if a leak is behind your spike.

A water bill that jumps without an obvious explanation is one of the most common reasons Fort Lauderdale homeowners call us. According to the EPA's WaterSense program, household leaks waste nearly one trillion gallons of water nationwide every year. The average home loses close to 9,400 gallons annually to leaks that often go unnoticed until the bill arrives.

Here's what we actually find most often when we're called out to investigate a spike.

The Most Common Culprits

A Running Toilet

A worn flapper or fill valve can waste water continuously, often silently enough that you never hear it running.

A Slab Leak

A leak under the concrete slab can run for weeks before it ever becomes visible above ground, especially common in Fort Lauderdale's slab-on-grade homes.

An Irrigation System Leak

A cracked line or a stuck valve on a sprinkler system can waste a significant amount of water. This is especially true if it runs on an automatic schedule you're not watching closely.

A Main Line Leak

A leak in the line between the meter and your home can waste water underground long before it's visible in your yard.

Pool Leaks And Irrigation: A Fort Lauderdale-Specific Wrinkle

South Florida's popularity for pools and lush landscaping adds two more common culprits that homeowners in drier climates rarely have to think about. A pool losing water more than about a quarter to half an inch per day, beyond what evaporation would explain, often points to a leak in the pool's own plumbing rather than the pool shell itself. Automatic irrigation systems are the other frequent offender. A cracked lateral line or a valve stuck open can run for days on a schedule nobody's actively watching, especially overnight.

Fort Lauderdale's sandy soil and high water table also mean ground settling around pool equipment pads and irrigation valve boxes. This happens more readily than in areas with more stable clay-based soil. That's part of why these specific leak sources show up here more often than national WaterSense averages would suggest for irrigation waste alone.

When A Leak Isn't The Answer

Not every unexplained bill increase traces back to a leak. A change in household size, a new appliance, or filling a pool for the first time can all explain a jump without anything being broken. So can a hotter, drier stretch of weather that increases outdoor watering. The meter test described above is the fastest way to rule leaks in or out. Use it before assuming the worst. Just as importantly, use it before assuming everything is fine when it isn't.

What A Professional Leak Detection Visit Actually Involves

If your own meter test confirms a leak but you can't find the source, a professional visit typically starts with the same basic principle at a more precise scale. A technician isolates sections of your plumbing system, sometimes using pressure gauges, to narrow down which zone is losing water. From there, acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging help pinpoint the exact location, whether that's a slab leak, a line under a driveway, or a fitting behind a wall. The goal at every step is confirming the location before anything gets opened, not guessing and hoping.

How To Narrow It Down Yourself

Before calling a plumber, there's a simple check you can do. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, then check your water meter. Wait about two hours without using any water, and check the meter again. If it moved, something in your system is leaking, even if you can't see it.

The toilet is worth checking specifically, since it's the most common source of a silent leak. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait about ten minutes without flushing. If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is letting water through and needs to be replaced.

Why Slab Leaks Are A Bigger Deal Here

Most South Florida homes are built slab-on-grade, meaning supply lines run directly under a concrete slab rather than through an accessible basement or crawlspace. When a line under the slab develops a slab leak, water can run for a long time without any visible sign beyond the water bill itself. Sometimes there's a warm spot on the floor if it's on the hot water line. Homes with original copper supply lines from several decades ago are the most common candidates. Copper pipe eventually develops small pinhole leaks from age. In coastal areas, salt air speeds up that corrosion even further.

FAQs

How much water can a running toilet actually waste?

A running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a day in some cases, which adds up fast over a billing cycle.

Is it worth getting a leak detection service instead of guessing?

If the meter test confirms a leak but you can't find it, yes. Electronic leak detection finds the source without unnecessary demolition.

Can the water utility tell me if I have a leak?

Some utilities flag unusually high usage, but they generally can't tell you where the leak is on your side of the meter. That's where a plumber's detection equipment comes in.
