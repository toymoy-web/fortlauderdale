Common Symptoms

What Usually Brings A Repair Call

No Hot Water

Often a failed element, thermostat, or, on gas units, a pilot or ignition issue.

Lukewarm Water

Frequently a sediment or sizing issue rather than full failure.

Leaking At The Base

Can mean a failed drain valve or fitting, or a corroded tank needing replacement instead.

Strange Noises

Popping or rumbling usually means sediment buildup, common with Fort Lauderdale's mineral content.

Before We Arrive

Water Heater Safety Checklist

Cut Power Or Gas First

If you smell gas or see sparking, shut off the gas supply or breaker before doing anything else, and leave the area if the gas smell is strong.

Don't Touch A Wet Electric Unit

Never touch an electric water heater or its breaker while standing in water on the floor around it.

Know Your Shutoff Valve

Locate the cold water shutoff valve at the top of the tank so you can stop the water supply if it's actively leaking.

Watch For Scalding Water

A malfunctioning thermostat can push water well past a safe temperature. Avoid using hot taps until the unit is checked if water feels unusually hot.

Salt air and humidity along the coast shorten the working life of heating elements and tank fittings compared to inland Florida homes. That's part of why a repair that would make sense on a 5-year-old inland unit sometimes doesn't on a similarly aged coastal one. We also see more sediment-related symptoms on units fed by well-influenced supply in some Broward County neighborhoods, which changes what a repair should actually target versus what it looks like on the surface. All repair work follows the water heater provisions of the Florida Building Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and manufacturer installation specifications.

Routine water heater repair is generally considered maintenance and isn't covered by homeowner's insurance. Sudden water damage from a tank failure is a separate, often-covered claim category, so it's worth documenting the failure with photos before cleanup begins if you plan to file a claim. Keep the failed part when possible, since some insurers and manufacturers ask to inspect it directly.

FAQs

Common Questions

Will you try to upsell me to a full replacement?

No. If a repair is the more cost-effective option, that's what we recommend.

Can a tankless unit be repaired instead of replaced?

Often yes, especially for a failed igniter, sensor, or descaling issue.

How do I know if it's worth repairing at all?

We weigh the repair cost against the unit's age and condition and give you a straight answer.

Can hard water make repairs happen more often?

Yes. Mineral buildup accelerates wear on heating elements and valves, which is part of why the same repair sometimes recurs sooner than the manufacturer's typical timeline suggests.

What if the unit is still under manufacturer warranty?

We'll check the warranty status before recommending a paid repair, since a part failure sometimes falls under coverage you're already entitled to.
