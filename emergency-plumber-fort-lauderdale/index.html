What Counts As An Emergency

When To Call The Emergency Line

Burst Or Actively Leaking Pipe

Water spreading through your home needs an immediate response, not a next-day appointment.

Sewage Backup

A backup affecting multiple drains or fixtures is both a plumbing and a health concern.

No Hot Water

A failed water heater on a weekend or holiday still gets the same 24/7 response.

Gas Odor

Any suspected gas leak should be treated as an emergency. Leave the area and call from a safe distance.

What Doesn't Need The Emergency Line

What Can Usually Wait For A Standard Appointment

A Slow Drain

A drain that's slower than usual but still draining can typically wait for a scheduled visit.

A Minor Faucet Drip

A drip that isn't actively causing damage can usually be scheduled during normal hours.

A Running Toilet

Wasteful but not damaging, a running toilet is worth fixing soon but rarely needs an emergency dispatch.

Routine Maintenance

Backflow testing, water heater descaling, and similar upkeep are scheduled, not emergency, work.

If you're not sure which category your issue falls into, call anyway. We'd rather help you triage it correctly than have you wait on something that actually needed immediate attention. A quick phone conversation costs nothing and can save you from either an unnecessary emergency dispatch or, worse, a delayed response to something that genuinely couldn't wait.

What To Do While You Wait

Immediate Steps That Help

Shut Off The Main Water Valve

Stopping water at the source limits damage while help is on the way.

Turn Off Power Near Standing Water

If water is near an outlet or panel, shut off power to that area from a dry location if you can do so safely.

Move Valuables Away From Water

Clearing furniture and belongings from the affected area reduces additional loss.

Don't Attempt A Gas Line Repair Yourself

Leave gas line work to a licensed technician; your job is to get clear and call.
