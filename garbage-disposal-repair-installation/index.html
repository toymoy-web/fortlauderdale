Common Disposal Problems

What Usually Brings A Repair Call

Jammed Or Stuck

Often cleared with a manual reset, though a foreign object lodged inside sometimes needs disassembly.

Leaking At The Base

Usually a worn seal or a cracked housing, which typically means replacement rather than repair.

Humming But Not Spinning

Points to a jammed impeller or a failed motor, diagnosed on site before quoting.

No Power At All

Can be a tripped reset button, a bad connection, or a fully failed unit.

Repair Vs. Replacement

When A New Unit Makes More Sense

Unit Older Than 10 Years

Disposals have a limited service life, and an older unit failing once often means more failures are coming.

Motor Burnout

A burned-out motor generally isn't cost-effective to repair compared to a new unit.

Frequent Jamming

A unit that jams repeatedly despite proper use may be undersized for the household's actual usage.

Upgrading Horsepower

Moving to a larger household or heavier kitchen use is a common reason to upgrade during a repair visit.

What Not To Put Down A Disposal

Avoiding The Next Jam

Grease And Fat

These solidify inside the line as they cool, narrowing the pipe over time even if the disposal itself handles them fine going down.

Fibrous Vegetables

Celery, corn husks, and similar fibrous scraps can wrap around the impeller and cause jams.

Coffee Grounds

They pass through easily but accumulate in the trap and pipe over time, contributing to slow drains.

Bones And Hard Shells

Most disposals aren't built to grind these, and forcing it shortens the motor's working life.

South Florida's water carries enough mineral content to accelerate wear on disposal components over time, similar to its effect on faucet cartridges, which is part of why a unit here sometimes needs replacement a bit sooner than the same model would in a different region.

FAQs

Common Questions

Can I just reset the disposal myself first?

Yes, pressing the reset button on the bottom of the unit is a safe first step. If it still won't run, that's when a repair call makes sense.

How long does installation take?

Most disposal installations are completed in under an hour, including removal of the old unit.

Do you install disposals that weren't originally there?

Yes, we can add a disposal to a kitchen that doesn't currently have one, checking that the drain and electrical setup support it.

Is a leak from the disposal always a big repair?

Not necessarily. A leak at the top seal is often a straightforward fix, while a crack in the housing usually means replacement.
