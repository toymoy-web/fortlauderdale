Common Toilet Problems

What We're Usually Called For

Running Constantly

Almost always a worn flapper or fill valve, an inexpensive fix that stops wasted water immediately.

Leaking At The Base

Often a failed wax ring underneath, which requires resetting the toilet to replace.

Weak Or Incomplete Flush

Can be a clogged jet, a low water level in the tank, or an older, inefficient toilet design.

Cracked Tank Or Bowl

A crack means replacement, not repair. We'll tell you plainly when that's the case.

When To Replace Instead

Repair Vs. Replacement

Older High-Flow Models

Toilets installed before modern low-flow standards use significantly more water per flush.

Repeated Repairs

If the same toilet keeps needing service, a new unit often costs less over a few years than continued repairs.

Cracked Porcelain

Any crack in the tank or bowl means the unit needs replacement, since it will eventually fail further.

Remodel Timing

If a bathroom remodel is already planned, replacing the toilet at the same time saves a separate service call later.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is a running toilet actually costing me money?

Yes, a constantly running toilet can waste a significant amount of water daily, which shows up directly on your water bill.

Can you match my existing toilet's bolt pattern?

In most cases yes, standard bolt patterns are common, and we'll confirm compatibility before ordering a unit.

How long does installation take?

Most toilet installations are completed within an hour or two, including removal of the old unit.

Do you offer water-efficient models?

Yes, we carry current low-flow options that meet modern water-efficiency standards.

Can an old toilet actually raise my water bill noticeably?

Yes. A pre-1994 high-flow model can use significantly more water per flush than a current low-flow unit, and that difference adds up across a household over a full billing cycle.
