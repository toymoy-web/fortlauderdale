When Replacement Is The Right Call

Repair Vs. Full Replacement

Fully Collapsed Sections

A pipe that has collapsed rather than just cracked generally can't be lined and needs replacement.

Multiple Failure Points

A line with damage in several separate spots often costs more to repair piece by piece than to replace outright.

Severe Offsets

Joints that have shifted significantly, not just cracked, are a strong signal that repair won't hold long-term.

Original Clay Or Orangeburg Pipe

Certain older pipe materials found in some historic Fort Lauderdale properties are generally past a reasonable repair point.

Replacement Methods

Trenchless Vs. Traditional Replacement

Pipe Bursting

A trenchless method that breaks apart the old pipe while pulling a new one into place, often without a full trench.

Traditional Open-Trench

Used when access, depth, or existing utilities rule out a trenchless approach.

Permit Handling

Sewer line replacement typically requires a permit, which we handle as part of the project.

Landscaping Consideration

We plan the approach to minimize damage to mature trees and established landscaping wherever the method allows.

Process

How Replacement Works

Confirm With Camera

We verify replacement is genuinely necessary rather than assuming it.

Choose A Method

Trenchless pipe bursting when the site allows it, traditional excavation when it doesn't.

Handle Permitting

Required permits are filed as part of the project timeline.

Replace And Test

The new line is installed, tested, and inspected before backfill or restoration.

Fort Lauderdale's mix of decades-old clay and cast iron sewer lines under mature, established landscaping makes trenchless pipe bursting a genuinely strong fit here more often than in newer subdivisions with less mature trees to protect. All work follows the plumbing provisions of the Florida Building Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Waterfront and canal-front properties sometimes add extra permitting steps depending on how close the line runs to a seawall, which we factor into the project timeline upfront rather than discovering it midway through the job.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I really need full replacement, or would a repair work?

We confirm with a camera inspection before recommending replacement, and we'll tell you honestly if repair or lining is a better fit.

Will replacing the line damage my landscaping?

We plan the method to minimize disruption, and trenchless pipe bursting in particular avoids a full trench in most cases.

How long does a sewer line replacement take?

A trenchless replacement is often completed in a single day; traditional excavation can take longer depending on length and restoration needs.

Do you handle the permit process?

Yes, required permits are filed and managed as part of the project.

What happens to my yard after the work is done?

We restore the affected area as part of the project scope, and a trenchless approach in particular leaves far less to restore than a full open trench.
