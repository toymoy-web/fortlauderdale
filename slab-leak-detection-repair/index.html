Signs Of A Slab Leak

What A Slab Leak Looks Like

Warm Spot On The Floor

A slab leak on the hot water line often creates a noticeably warm patch on tile or flooring.

Unexplained Water Bill Increase

A leak under the slab can run continuously without any visible sign above ground.

Sound Of Running Water

Hearing water movement in the floor or walls when everything is off is a strong indicator.

Cracked Flooring Or Baseboards

Sustained moisture from a slab leak can eventually cause flooring or baseboards to shift or crack.

Why It Happens Here

Fort Lauderdale's Slab-On-Grade Risk

Slab-On-Grade Construction

Most South Florida homes run supply lines directly under a concrete slab, common throughout the region.

Copper Pipe Age

Original copper lines installed decades ago are reaching the point where pinhole leaks become more likely.

Salt Air Corrosion

Homes closer to the coast and canal system see faster corrosion on copper lines than inland properties.

Soil Shifting

Ground movement can stress a line at a joint over years, eventually leading to a leak or crack.

If You Suspect A Slab Leak

What To Do Before We Arrive

Note What You've Noticed

Write down when you first saw the warm spot, heard the sound, or noticed the bill increase. That timeline helps with diagnosis.

Don't Cut Into The Slab Yourself

Attempting to locate or expose a slab leak without detection equipment risks damaging the wrong section of flooring and structure.

Watch For Mold Or Musty Odor

Sustained moisture under flooring can lead to mold growth. A musty smell alongside a suspected leak is worth mentioning when you call.

Know Your Main Shutoff

If the leak is significant, shutting off the main water valve limits further water loss until a technician arrives.

Repair Vs. Reroute

Which Fix Makes More Sense

In-Slab Repair

  • Keeps the original pipe path.
  • Requires breaking through the slab at the leak point.
  • Best for a single, accessible leak location.
  • Flooring restoration is needed at the repair site.

Above-Slab Reroute

  • Avoids breaking through concrete entirely.
  • New line runs through walls or attic space instead.
  • Often the better call for older, widely corroded copper.
  • Some new pipe may be visible depending on the home's layout.

Fort Lauderdale's slab-on-grade construction combined with decades-old copper supply lines in many established neighborhoods creates a genuinely higher rate of slab leaks than newer inland Florida subdivisions with more recent plumbing. Detection and repair follow the plumbing provisions of the Florida Building Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

FAQs

Common Questions

Do you have to break through my floor to find the leak?

No, detection is done electronically first. Any opening is limited to the confirmed leak location, not exploratory.

Is rerouting better than repairing in place?

It depends on the leak's location and your flooring. Rerouting above the slab avoids breaking concrete but adds some visible pipe.

Can one slab leak mean there are others coming?

If the line is old copper and one section has failed from corrosion, nearby sections are worth monitoring, since they've seen similar wear.

How fast can someone come out?

Slab leaks are treated as a priority given the ongoing water loss and potential flooring damage, with same-day dispatch in most cases.
