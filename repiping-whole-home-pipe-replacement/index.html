Signs It's Time To Repipe

When Repeated Repairs Stop Making Sense

Repeated Pinhole Leaks

Multiple leaks in different spots on the same copper line usually means the pipe itself is failing, not just one section.

Persistent Low Pressure

Corrosion narrowing old galvanized or cast iron pipe from the inside often can't be fixed by replacing one section.

Discolored Water Sitewide

Rust-colored water at multiple fixtures points to pipe material failure, not a single bad fitting.

Home Older Than 50 Years

Original supply lines in Fort Lauderdale's historic neighborhoods are approaching or past their expected service life.

What Repiping Involves

Whole-Home Vs. Partial Repiping

Whole-Home Repiping

Every supply line in the home is replaced, typically with PEX or copper, eliminating recurring failures at the source.

Partial Repiping

Only the affected section or floor is replaced when the rest of the system is still sound.

Access Planning

We plan access points to minimize wall and ceiling openings wherever possible.

Material Choice

PEX and copper each have trade-offs in cost, flexibility, and longevity that we walk through before you decide.

Process

How A Repiping Project Works

Assess The System

We inspect existing pipe material, age, and condition throughout the home.

Plan Access Points

Access is planned to minimize the amount of wall or ceiling that needs opening.

Replace The Lines

New supply lines are run and connected, section by section or in a full pass.

Test And Patch

The system is pressure-tested before any opened wall or ceiling is patched.

Older Fort Lauderdale homes near the coast and canal system see corrosion progress faster on original copper and galvanized lines than the same materials would inland, which is part of why a repipe decision that could wait 5-10 more years elsewhere sometimes can't here. Homes in Victoria Park, Colee Hammock, and Rio Vista built before the 1970s are the profile we see this come up for most often. All new piping and fittings meet the plumbing provisions of the Florida Building Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

FAQs

Common Questions

Do you have to open every wall in the house?

No. We plan access points strategically and patch as we go, aiming to minimize openings.

Is PEX or copper better for my home?

It depends on your budget, water chemistry, and how long you plan to stay in the home. We walk through the trade-offs before you decide.

Can repiping be done in stages instead of all at once?

Yes, a partial repipe by section or floor is an option when the whole system doesn't need immediate replacement.

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

Most single-family homes take a few days from start to finish, depending on size and how much access planning is needed.

Do we need to move out during the work?

Most homeowners stay in the home during a repipe, though water will be shut off in sections at a time as work progresses.
