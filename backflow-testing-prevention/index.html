Who Needs Backflow Testing

Requirements In Fort Lauderdale And Broward County

Irrigation Systems

Homes with an irrigation system typically have a backflow preventer that requires annual testing.

Commercial Properties

Most commercial properties are required to test backflow assemblies annually to maintain water service.

Pool Fill Lines

A dedicated backflow device is often required where a pool fill line connects to the potable water supply.

Fire Sprinkler Systems

Fire suppression systems typically have their own backflow preventer subject to the same testing requirement.

Why It Matters

Protecting Fort Lauderdale's Drinking Water

Cross-Connection Risk

A backflow preventer stops contaminated water from an irrigation system, pool, or industrial process from siphoning back into the drinking water supply during a pressure drop.

Canal-Adjacent Properties

Homes with irrigation drawn from canal water alongside a municipal connection carry a genuinely higher cross-connection risk that makes a properly functioning device especially important.

Device Types

Different assemblies, from simple vacuum breakers to reduced-pressure zone devices, are required depending on the level of hazard the connection presents.

Common Failure Points

Worn rubber seals and debris caught in the check valve are the most common reasons a device fails its annual test.

Broward County's backflow testing requirements apply broadly across residential irrigation systems and commercial properties. A missed annual test can result in water service being restricted until compliance is restored, worth avoiding by scheduling ahead of the deadline. Canal-adjacent properties in particular should treat this testing seriously given the cross-connection risks unique to that setup. Device selection and testing follow the backflow provisions of the Florida Building Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

FAQs

Common Questions

How often does backflow testing need to happen?

Most Broward County jurisdictions require annual testing for irrigation, commercial, and fire suppression backflow assemblies.

What happens if my device fails the test?

We can typically repair or replace it the same visit and retest before you leave.

Who receives the test results?

We file required certification paperwork with your utility or municipality as part of the service.

Can a residential homeowner test their own device?

Testing must be performed and certified by a licensed tester, so a DIY test does not satisfy the requirement.

What triggers a re-inspection outside the annual schedule?

A change in the connection type, a repair to the device, or a reported issue can all trigger a re-test outside the normal annual cycle.
