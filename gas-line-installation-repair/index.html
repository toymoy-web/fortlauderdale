If You Smell Gas

What To Do Immediately

Leave The Area

If the smell is strong, get everyone out of the house immediately, don't investigate further.

Don't Use Switches Or Flames

Avoid light switches, appliances, or anything that could spark near a suspected gas leak.

Call From Outside

Call us, or your gas utility's emergency line, once you're safely away from the building.

Don't Try To Fix It Yourself

Gas line work requires licensed handling. Wait for a professional rather than attempting a repair.

Many Fort Lauderdale homes built before the 1990s were plumbed for gas ranges or water heaters that have since been swapped to electric. Reactivating or extending an old gas line in these homes often needs a fresh inspection rather than assuming the existing line is still sound. A line that's sat unused for years can develop corrosion at fittings just like an active one. All gas line work follows the fuel gas provisions of the Florida Building Code, based on NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is it safe to keep using appliances if I smell a faint gas odor?

No. Any gas odor, faint or strong, should be treated seriously. Leave the area and call for service rather than waiting to see if it gets worse.

Can you run a new line for an outdoor kitchen or grill?

Yes, we run and properly size new gas lines for outdoor appliances, tested before use.

Do you handle both natural gas and propane?

Yes, we work with both fuel types and size lines accordingly.

How do you test a line after repair?

Every repaired or new line is pressure-tested to confirm there are no leaks before it's approved for use.

Can an old, unused gas line be reactivated safely?

Sometimes, but it needs a fresh inspection and pressure test first. A line that's sat unused for years can develop corrosion at fittings that isn't obvious from the outside.
