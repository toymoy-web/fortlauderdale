Who Actually Needs One Here

Sump Pumps In A Slab-On-Grade City

Most Fort Lauderdale homes are built slab-on-grade, so a basement sump pump setup common up north is rare here. That doesn't mean sump pumps are irrelevant locally. A meaningful number of properties still depend on one, just for different reasons than a northern basement would.

Pool Equipment Pits

A below-grade equipment pad or pit near a pool can collect groundwater, especially on a lot with a high water table.

French Drain Systems

Some yards use a French drain to manage runoff, and that drain often terminates at a sump pump and basin.

Older Crawlspace Homes

A smaller number of older Fort Lauderdale homes were built with a crawlspace rather than a slab, and these can need a pump during heavy rain.

Canal-Adjacent Low Points

A low-lying corner of a canal-front lot can take on water during a king tide or heavy storm, even without flooding the home itself.

Why Pumps Fail Here Specifically

The Timing Problem

A sump pump's job is to run hardest exactly when conditions are worst: during a tropical storm or a sustained downpour. That's also when a power outage is most likely. A pump with no backup power simply stops at the moment it's needed most, which is the single most common failure pattern we see during Fort Lauderdale's hurricane season.

Float switch failure is the other common cause. The float that tells the pump when to turn on can stick, especially if sediment or debris has built up in the basin over time without routine checking.

Backup Power Options

Keeping It Running When The Power Doesn't

Battery Backup

A battery backup system takes over automatically during an outage, buying time until power returns or the storm passes.

Water-Powered Backup

Where municipal water pressure allows it, a water-powered backup needs no battery at all and can run as long as water pressure holds.

High-Water Alarms

A simple battery-powered alarm won't pump water, but it gives an early warning if the basin level is rising faster than expected.

Installing backup power is especially worth considering ahead of hurricane season rather than during an active storm watch, when parts and scheduling both get harder to line up on short notice.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do Fort Lauderdale homes actually need sump pumps if most are slab-on-grade?

Many do not, but a meaningful number here do: properties with a below-grade pool equipment pit, a French drain system managing yard runoff, an older home with a crawlspace, or a canal-adjacent lot with a low-lying corner all commonly rely on one.

Why does mine keep failing during hurricane season specifically?

Sump pumps work hardest exactly when the power is most likely to go out. A unit that runs constantly during a tropical storm, then loses power at the worst moment, is the single most common failure pattern we see here.

Do you install battery backup systems?

Yes. A battery or water-powered backup keeps the pump running through a power outage, which is often the exact moment it is needed most during a storm.

How do I know if my sump pump is failing before it actually stops working?

A pump that runs constantly even in dry weather, one that short-cycles on and off rapidly, or one that has started making a grinding or rattling noise are all early warning signs worth a service call.

Is sump pump failure covered by homeowner's insurance?

Typically not on its own, since pump failure is treated as mechanical wear. Water damage that results from the failure may be covered depending on your specific policy. Check your policy's sump pump exclusion language.
